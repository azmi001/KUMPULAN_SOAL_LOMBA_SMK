Historically, hemp was one of the materials available for ropes, including climbing ropes, due to its strength and durability. However, it has several drawbacks that make it less suitable for safety-critical applications like climbing:
1	Stretchability: Hemp ropes have low elasticity, which means they do not absorb the shock well during a fall, potentially leading to a more dangerous situation for climbers.
2	Weight and Handling: Hemp ropes are generally heavier and less flexible compared to modern synthetic ropes, making them cumbersome and difficult to handle during climbing.
3	Deterioration: While hemp is durable in many environments, it is susceptible to wear and environmental damage over time, such as rot and mildew if not properly cared for, which can compromise safety.

Highly engineered materials
Today, climbing ropes are primarily made from highly engineered synthetic materials like nylon, which offer superior strength, elasticity, and durability under various environmental conditions. These materials provide better shock absorption and are more reliable for the dynamic and often extreme conditions encountered in climbing.
